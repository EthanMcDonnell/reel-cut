# Voice Profile — how you actually talk

Generated from the full transcripts of your top-performing reels via
`/voice-profile`. Read this before writing a script body so the output matches
your real register, not generic AI explainer voice. Every trait below is drawn
from your own videos; quoted fragments are real (lightly cleaned of ASR errors).

## Pacing & length
- **~3.1 words/second** spoken (range 2.6–4.0). A 50–60s reel is ~150–185 spoken
  words — which is exactly the scripts skill's ~175-word target. Don't pad past it.
- Top performers run **130–210 body words**; the two engagement monsters sit at
  140 and 210, so length isn't the lever — the hook and CTA are.

## Openers (sentence 1 of the body, after the hook)
You favor a casual connector or a flat declarative that sounds like you're
mid-thought, never a formal thesis statement:
- `"So Netflix literally ships free servers to ISPs around the world."`
- `"So Cloudflare literally uses lava lamps..."`
- `"ChatGPT serves 800 million users from a single Postgres database."`
- `"GitHub just raised the price of Copilot by 900%."`
"So" opens roughly 1 in 4. **"literally"** is your signature disbelief intensifier
("literally ships", "literally uses lava lamps") — use it sparingly for the absurd
reveal, not as filler.

## Sentence rhythm
- Short declaratives carry the reveals; you undercut immediately for tension:
  `"It's a 42kb file. That's it. Or at least that's what it seems."`
- You vary length but keep the spine tight — no long subordinate pile-ups. When a
  mechanism needs steps, you chain short sentences, each adding one piece:
  `"Inside are more zip files. Inside those, more zip files. Layers deep, each one
  hiding more compressed archives than the last."`

## Vocabulary & emotion
Real feeling words you use (use these, don't invent new ones):
- **"wild"** — `"The economics are wild."`
- **"nightmare"** — `"Facebook's ads app was a nightmare."`
- **"crashed and burned"**, **"the clever part"**, **"the trick is"**, **"the hard
  part was"**, **"almost boring"** (`"The fix is almost boring."`).
Plain and concrete over technical-neutral. Numbers stated bluntly, no hedging.

## Address
- Direct **"you / your"** when the topic touches the viewer:
  `"your bill on June 1st could be nine times higher"`,
  `"it is likely bricking your PC"`,
  `"when you're watching Stranger Things, you're probably streaming from a server
  in your city"`.
- Light **"I"** for personal credibility, used once, not throughout:
  `"I find the status bar script that I use super helpful for tracking this."`

## Reveal & transition phrases (open/close loops)
These are your real connective tissue — reuse them to hold tension:
- `"But here's what makes [X] different..."`
- `"But here is what actually makes this work."`
- `"So here's the clever part."`
- `"So how do you solve this chicken-and-egg problem?"`
- `"That's exactly what [X] exploited."`

## Closers / CTA style
You almost always land a one-line reframe and/or a witty follow:
- **Signature reframe:** `"Sometimes the best engineering solution is shipping
  heaps and heaps of hard drives all over the world."` /
  `"...is to just go buy lava lamps."` /
  `"Some just need math."` — a callback that recasts the whole video.
- **Witty kicker follows:** `"or just buy a Mac."` /
  `"Follow or we'll have to learn Angular."` /
  `"Follow for more tech behind Big Tech."`
- **Comment-bait** (your single highest-engagement move):
  `Comment "Claude" and I'll send you [the script].` Use when there's a real lead
  magnet (see proven-hooks.md — this drove 3000+ comments on your #1 video).

## Never do (absent from your real speech)
Cross-check with the `tellcheck` skill, and specifically avoid what you never say:
- No "In today's video" / "Let's dive in" / "Buckle up" throat-clearing.
- No "it's important to note", "delve", "leverage", "in conclusion".
- No em dashes in the spoken line.
- No abstract thesis opener — you start mid-thought with a concrete fact.
